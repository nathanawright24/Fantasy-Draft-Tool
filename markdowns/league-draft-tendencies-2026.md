## 1. The league-wide shape of the first 8 rounds

Pooled position mix by round, all four main-league drafts:

| Round | QB | RB | WR | TE |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0% | 52% | 46% | 2% |
| 2 | 4% | 42% | 48% | 6% |
| 3 | 15% | 40% | 35% | 10% |
| 4 | 19% | 21% | 44% | 15% |
| 5 | 15% | 21% | 44% | 19% |
| 6 | 17% | 29% | 44% | 10% |
| 7 | 8% | 23% | 54% | 15% |
| 8 | 12% | 35% | 42% | 10% |

Four structural facts:

**1. Round 1 is RB/WR only, always.** 48 first-round picks across four years: 25 RB, 22 WR, 1 TE (Kaiden taking Kelce at 1.8 in 2023). Zero QBs, ever. In 2025 the first 16 picks were a pure RB/WR alternation before Bowers went 17th.

**2. Round 3 is the QB round, and then the market dies.** In 2025, QB1–QB5 went at picks 25, 27, 33, 35, and 39 — five quarterbacks in a 15-pick window starting exactly at the round 3 turn. Then picks 40 through 99 produced only **four** quarterbacks (55, 71, 78, 85). This is the single most exploitable pattern in the league: there is no penalty for skipping the round 3 run, because the drop-off between QB6 and QB11 is far smaller than the 60-pick gap suggests. The same shape holds in 2022–2024 (QB1 at 17, 20, and 28 respectively, then a similar mid-round vacuum).

**3. TE has two cliffs, not one.** 2025: Bowers at 17, McBride at 31, then a pause, then Kittle and Kelce at 49–50, then a flood — TE5 through TE10 all came off between picks 67 and 84 (LaPorta, Hockenson, Engram, Andrews, Njoku, Warren). Six tight ends in an 18-pick window. Practical read: either take one of the top two by pick ~31, or plan to strike in the 67–75 zone, but **do not** be TE-less after pick 84.

**4. Round 8 is the last real RB window.** Rounds 8 and 9 in 2025 produced 11 running backs. If you're thin at RB entering round 8, that's your last chance at anyone with a defined role.

**Kickers:** seven of eleven kickers went in round 13 (picks 146–155). The lone exception is Cailen, who took Brandon Aubrey at **10.10 (pick 118)** — 28 picks before any other kicker. Joseph drafted no kicker at all in 2025. You can comfortably wait until round 14+; Asa took kickers in rounds 15 and 16 across his two drafts, the latest in the league.

## 5. What to do with this in 2026

1. **Never spend a round 1 pick on a QB or TE.** 48 first-round picks, four years, one TE and zero QBs. Anyone who deviates is buying at a premium nobody else in the league will match.

2. **The QB decision is binary and it happens at pick ~25–39 — but 2026 scoring changes the math.** Greg (rounds 3/3/4/4), Nick (3/4/4/6), Asa (3/4), Cailen (3/5), and Kaiden (3/6/6/6, trending earlier) are your confirmed early-QB managers, and exactly five QBs were gone by pick 39 in 2025 — that's not a coincidence, it's those five people. **Colin belongs on the watch list too:** he punted to round 9 in your league but took Hurts in round 3 in the alt draft, so he's willing.

   🚨 **Read this before acting on the paragraph above.** The league added 5-point passing TDs and a 3-point 300-yard passing bonus **for 2026**. Every draft in this dataset predates that. Under the old scoring, the correct play was to skip the round 3 run, because picks 40–99 yielded only four QBs — a round 7–9 QB cost you almost nothing. **That is no longer safe advice.** The new scoring widens the gap between the top QB and a replacement-level starter by roughly twice as much as it widens the gap among flex players, which makes paying early for a QB genuinely more defensible than this historical data suggests. Expect an earlier and larger QB run than the five-by-pick-39 pattern, and treat the size of the overcorrection as unknown. Kaiden has already shown he adapts (round 6 → round 3 in one offseason); Ryan's round-7 metronome and Nathan's drift toward round 9–10 are the tendencies most likely to break this year.

3. **TE: the 67–84 window is where the league panics.** Six TEs went in 18 picks. If you want Bowers/McBride-tier, you pay by pick 31. If you don't, target pick 65–72 and let Ryan, Cailen, Greg, Kaiden, and Tyler fight over the leftovers behind you.

4. **Kicker: wait.** Round 13 is the herd. Cailen is the only one who'll take one before round 12, and no kicker outside him left the board before pick 146.

5. **RB scarcity peaks in round 8.** Eleven RBs in rounds 8–9. Joseph is your most consistent early-RB competitor (3.0 average in R1–8, and he opened RB in three of four years); Nathan and Asa (4–5 WR each, every draft) are not. Dylan is average on RB despite how 2025 looked.

   Also worth pricing in: **Dylan removes two QBs and two-to-three TEs from the pool every year.** If you're planning to punt QB into round 9+, remember he's taking a second one in the round 10–12 range, and Nathan, Greg, Nick, Kaiden, and Cailen all take a late second QB too. The bottom of the QB pool is thinner than it looks.

6. **Bias picks to bank on.** Nick will take the top Bear roughly a round early and then hoard Chicago depth after round 10. Colin takes Giants without hesitation — he spent 1.8 on Nabers. Cailen pays mid-round prices for Broncos; Kaiden pays late-round prices for them. Nathan takes Jaguars within a round of value. Asa and Jayden, despite their Vikings and Giants listings, have not actually done this across two drafts each — model them as unbiased. Ryan and Dylan's Steelers lean only shows up on kickers and TEs, because that's what Pittsburgh has.

7. **The new scoring mildly undercuts the league's WR-first habit.** With PPR already baked in, the 2026 additions favor QBs most (+35 to +59 points), then workhorse RBs (+30, and dual-role backs clear 100 scrimmage yards via rush + receiving combined), then alpha WRs (+18), then TEs (+12 for an elite one). Two flex spots in PPR still make WR depth correct — don't abandon it — but the marginal edge shifts toward workhorse backs and quarterbacks. Nathan's WR-max build (4–5 receivers in the first eight rounds, every draft) is the strategy most exposed by the change.

8. **Corollary for you:** if you want a player on Chicago, Denver, New York, or Jacksonville, you cannot wait for value — there are two Bears fans, two Broncos fans, and two Giants fans in a 12-team league. Conversely, Vikings, Falcons, and Steelers players tend to fall past their market price here.
